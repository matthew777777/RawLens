LK-R Lite parity with the PC solver and the two papers

Sources
  2104.06191v2, Lucas-Kanade Reloaded, PDF pp. 3–5, Eqs. 1–7.
  2207.14671v1, High Dynamic Range and Super-Resolution from Raw Image
  Bursts, PDF pp. 5–8, Eqs. 2, 4, 6–12.
  PC source means the local LK-Reloaded-Super-Resolution checkout.
  Lite source means tools/lkr-lite in RawLens.

Execution order and code map

1. Coarse registration and initialization
   2021 §3.4 uses forward-additive LK on bilinear-demosaiced grayscale.
   2022 §4.2 starts with FFT alignment and an aligned, exposure-weighted
   demosaiced mean. Section 4.3 refines registration on learned features.
   PC lkr_full.cpp:238 selects bilinear grayscale in paper mode. Production
   uses binned grayscale, coarse and tile LK, plus optional local search.
   Lite lkrl_full.cpp:297 estimates coarse motion and :614 starts tile LK.
   lkrl_solver.cpp:542 builds the RCD seed. Line 800 schedules one or three
   leading motion stages at 1× or 2×, inserts the weighted RAW average A,
   and runs three or eight data updates. The average also guides the finish.
   A coherent MHC seed gave +0.24–0.29 dB PC agreement at 1× on the two
   crops, but the measured merge times did not justify replacing RCD.

2. Forward operator and validity
   2021 Eqs. 1–2 warp, integrate over a sensor pixel and pick its Bayer
   channel. 2022 Eq. 4 additionally multiplies by exposure and clips.
   PC lkr_solver.cpp:413 dispatches its FP32 residual and adjoint unless a
   packed variant is selected. Its production warp has local translations
   on top of the tile affine.
   Lite shaders/lkr/resid.comp:24 computes the same affine plus interpolated
   local translations, bilinear taps and s² average in the temporal pass.
   The residual sign is Uz − y. An invalid or clipped sample gets zero
   residual and zero validity. A valid, exact-zero residual still gets
   validity one. RAW storage is packed FP16. Arithmetic and confidence
   buffers are FP32. The scatter sums use signed 32-bit fixed point.
   The pass now emits validity while it already has the taps. The solver
   reuses that mask from lkrl_solver.cpp:690 onward, avoiding the second
   traversal. Confidence rereads the original residual where an optional
   residual guide requires it.

3. Confidence, saturation and exposure
   2021 Eq. 5 uses an unweighted least-squares data term.
   2022 Eqs. 6–7 and 12 use a saturation mask and exposure normalization
   across frames, multiplied by learned image confidence. Eq. 9 then uses
   squared weights. With e relative to the chosen exposure and y'=y/e,
   the effective coefficient on (Uz − y') is e² w².
   PC lkr_solver.cpp:620 computes temporal confidence on the reference
   seed and :413 combines it with the guide and minimum spread. Paper
   mode disables this handcrafted confidence. The tested PC CLI has
   no exposure-bracket argument corresponding to Lite's --hdr 1.
   Lite lkrl_solver.cpp:708 computes detail tolerance, including a local
   motion-curvature gate. It sums row statistics with weight_th.comp,
   runs the temporal test with weight_t.comp, and optionally applies the
   wider shadow test with weight_tv.comp and weight_td.comp. Then
   weight_guide.comp uses Bayer-quad color statistics, weight_minh.comp
   and weight_min.comp spread rejection, and wvalid.comp:39 applies
   validity and any exposure factor.
   Lite lkrl_full.cpp:653 converts observations to reference-exposure units.
   wvalid.comp:17 uses a relative inverse-variance factor
      (a y' + b) / (a y'/e + b/e²) / max(e²).
   It also bypasses motion rejection near a clipped reference where a
   shorter exposure supplies usable data. Those are separate modeling
   choices. With fixed confidence and all frames valid, the normalized
   Eq. 9 and Lite coefficients differed by mean absolute 0.207 in the
   synthetic bracket check. Replacing one formula with the other would
   change the objective and needs a separate quality justification.

4. Image update
   2021 Eq. 5 uses Uᵀ(Uz − y) plus µ(z − x), scaled by learned η.
   2022 Eq. 9 uses Aᵀ[w²(Az − y)] plus its HQS coupling term.
   PC lkr_solver.cpp:602 computes µ and its estimated 1/(L+µ) step size.
   Lite shaders/adj.comp:69 evaluates the weighted residual, sends it
   back through the same taps, and produces UᵀW1 coverage. zupd.comp:84
   uses a per-pixel coverage-dependent approximation to multiple PC
   updates. Lite uses independent RGB at 1× and a coupled 2× fit around
   lkrl_solver.cpp:941. The final hybrid filter is at shaders/hyb.comp:18.
   The simpler independent 2× fit improves agreement with PC on the
   Paris and car crops by 1.15 and 1.96 dB, respectively. On labelled
   synthetic rail pixels it lowers agreement with ground truth from
   21.72 to 20.95 dB. Radius one increases chroma error on that target.
   Retaining radius two preserves the current anti-zipper behavior.

5. Motion and prior
   2021 Eq. 7 solves affine normal equations against updated z, then the
   ResUNet produces x for the next stage. The 2022 paper replaces image
   registration features and confidence with learned features and uses
   learned HQS stages for the HDR model.
   PC lkr_solver.cpp:641 interleaves motion and z updates and :770 runs
   the external prior inside the loop.
   Lite lkrl_solver.cpp:980 uses leading Gauss–Newton updates, with tile
   affine and local block reductions in jac.comp and jacblk.comp. An
   external learned prior runs once after each tile in lkrl_adapter.cpp:312.
   The two local TorchScript adapters form the same FP32 1×4×H×W tensor
   from planar RGB and sqrt(0.18a + b), then run CPU inference. The
   experimentally trained local checkpoint can therefore be shared,
   but its input changes with the fit and its feedback schedule.

Measured checks

- 2021 L2 equation check, archived Vulkan PC solver versus the FP32 Torch
  oracle, Paris 20-frame 96×96 crop at 2×, four stages, one data step per
  stage. 137.6 dB, RGB RMS 5.93e-8, maximum affine translation difference
  2.53e-5 LR pixels.
- Lite operator replay, four PC input traces from Paris and car, 1× and 2×.
  Lite produced its geometry, confidence and seed. The separate CPU
  adjoint held those fixed. RMS gradient difference was 8.77e-8 to
  4.26e-7 with matching FP16 RAW staging, versus 3.84e-6 to 3.30e-5 with
  original FP32 RAW. Almost all of the additional first-step error is
  explained by RAW storage precision. That test does not equate the two
  confidence maps or compare the complete trained models.
- 2022 Eq. 9, synthetic FP32 exposure bracket, analytic transpose versus
  PyTorch autograd. Maximum absolute errors 5.96e-8 at 1× and 1.49e-8 at 2×.
- Same local FP32 TorchScript prior on a 320×256 crop at 2×, eight Paris
  frames, desktop CPU prior on both sides. PC used 8 prior calls in
  4,096 ms total merge, Lite used 2 in 2,666 ms. Lite without the prior
  took 162 ms. Agreement to the learned PC output was 43.39 dB with the
  shared checkpoint and 43.33 dB without the Lite checkpoint. This used
  local experimental weights. The PDFs describe training and architecture.
- No-prior PC/Lite camera-linear comparison, same eight frames and
  768×640 sensor crop, 16 output pixels excluded per boundary, peak 1.
    Paris 1×   49.07 dB       Car 1×   51.34 dB
    Paris 2×   44.59 dB       Car 2×   47.78 dB
- Fused Lite path produced byte-identical float output for all four crops,
  the full Paris DCT run, a synthetic ±2 EV bracket and five alternate
  confidence configurations. Five-run desktop medians for the four crops
  were Paris 1× 262 → 223 ms, Paris 2× 494 → 479 ms, car 1× 260 → 220 ms
  and car 2× 465 → 452 ms. Full Paris 20-frame 1× DCT, 4032×3024,
  averaged 8.708 → 8.384 s over two alternating runs. Dispatches fell from
  6,400 to 6,320. No Android device was connected for timing.

Reproduction
  Enable LKR_TRACE_PREFIX on the PC CLI and select a tile trace.
  Pass the resulting prefix to the Lite host runner:
    lkrl_run --trace PC_TILE_PREFIX output.f32 --audit-prefix LITE_TRACE --no-prior
    python tools/lkr-lite/research/check_data_step.py LITE_TRACE
  The full run logs and arrays for this audit are in /tmp/lkrl-parity-fp32/.
